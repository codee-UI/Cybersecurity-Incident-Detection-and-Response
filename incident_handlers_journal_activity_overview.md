# Incident Handler's Journal

## Activity 1: Documenting a Security Incident

**Date:** October 14, 2026  
**Entry:** #1

### Description
Documenting a ransomware incident at a small U.S. health care clinic. The incident began after targeted phishing emails containing malicious attachments enabled attackers to deploy ransomware and encrypt critical files, including medical records. The disruption prevented employees from accessing essential files and software and forced the clinic to shut down its computer systems and seek technical assistance.

### Tool(s) Used
None specified in the scenario. No cybersecurity investigation tools were identified as having been used during the incident.

### The 5 W's

**Who caused the incident?**  
An organized group of unethical hackers caused the incident. The scenario describes the group as targeting organizations in the health care and transportation industries.

**What happened?**  
The attackers deployed ransomware that encrypted the clinic's critical files, including medical records. Employees could not access the files and software needed for their work, and a ransom note demanded a large sum of money in exchange for the decryption key.

**When did the incident occur?**  
The incident was reported on a Tuesday at approximately 9:00 a.m. The scenario does not provide a specific calendar date.

**Where did the incident happen?**  
The incident occurred in the computer systems and network of a small health care clinic in the United States that provides primary-care services.

**Why did the incident happen?**  
The attackers gained access through targeted phishing emails sent to several employees. The emails contained malicious attachments that installed malware when downloaded, allowing the attackers to deploy ransomware; the ransom demand suggests a financial motive.

### Additional Notes
1. How can the clinic improve phishing awareness, email filtering, endpoint protection, access controls, and backup procedures to reduce the risk of another ransomware incident?
2. The clinic should preserve relevant evidence, isolate affected systems, and coordinate with qualified incident responders. It should assess safe recovery options and legal or regulatory reporting obligations before making decisions about the ransom.
3. Further investigation is needed to determine the scope of the compromise, whether data was exfiltrated, how the attackers moved through the network, and whether clean backups are available.
